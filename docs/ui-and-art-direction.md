# UI and art direction

Delivered UI and art-direction work is recorded in [archive/ui-and-art-direction-completed.md](archive/ui-and-art-direction-completed.md). This live document carries only the art and asset work that remains.

The production standard itself is [art-bible.md](art-bible.md). It is the reference, not a to-do list; what follows is what still has to be done to meet it.

## Where the art stands

Measured, not estimated. The numbers come from [asset-report.md](asset-report.md) and [player-frame-geometry.md](player-frame-geometry.md), regenerated by `npm run report:assets` and `npm run report:art`.

- 167 shipped images totalling **38.1 MB**, against a target of roughly 6 MB.
- **83** of those breach a role budget.
- **13** exact-duplicate groups and **9** orphaned assets.
- **7** backgrounds are non-uniformly stretched because their aspect is not the stage's 1.857.
- **0** of 167 shipped assets carry a recorded provenance or licence.

The player's scale is no longer part of this list. It is calibrated per room and capped by an assertion, and is recorded in the art bible and the archive. **NPC scale still is**: three human NPCs are drawn at a different scale from the player standing beside them — the carpenter at 2.65x, the woman who lost her mirror at 1.74x, and the librarian at 0.57x (BUG-044).

## Outstanding work

### Normalise the room art

Rooms are still in several visual languages at once: differing hand-drawn and painted treatments, differing perspective and detail levels, and one room — River Crossing — considerably more polished than its neighbours. The order of work is:

1. Fix the stretched backgrounds (BUG-041). A distorted room cannot be judged for anything else, so this comes first.
2. Repaint the Den from the standard camera (BUG-042). It is the one room whose painted scale contradicts the rest of the game.
3. Choose and approve one gold-standard room and one player/NPC interaction as the target the rest are normalised towards. **Leigh's decision; this cannot be automated.**
4. Normalise room by room against that target. Do not ship a scene with partially restyled elements in it.
5. Add visual baselines only once a scene is approved, so the tests protect intentional art rather than accidental inconsistency.

### Finish the player character

The player is the most-seen asset in the game and is currently three different drawings.

- Redraw the side idle poses on the walk canvas, in the walk finish (BUG-039).
- Author the missing front and back walk frames (BUG-040).
- Give the player a contact shadow. Correct scaling still reads as floating without one.

### Bring the assets inside budget

- Re-export painted scenery as WebP. PNG is the wrong container for it and is most of the 38.1 MB.
- Resolve the 13 duplicate groups by aliasing to one semantic ID rather than shipping copies.
- Give inventory icons their own crops instead of reusing the world sprite at 660x567 for a 48 px slot.
- Decide what to do with the 9 orphans and the 31.5 MB of unshipped reference material in `resources/imageDump/`.

### Record provenance

Every shipped asset needs its `provenance` and `licence` fields filled in. The manifest leaves them `null` so an unrecorded asset stays visible; nothing ships with an unresolved licence question.

## Tools

| Command | What it produces |
| --- | --- |
| `npm run report:assets` | the manifest and [asset-report.md](asset-report.md): roles, dimensions, bytes, hashes, budget breaches, duplicates, orphans |
| `npm run check:assets` | the same, exiting non-zero on a budget breach |
| `npm run report:art` | per-room scale calibration sheets, the all-room montage, contact sheets per role, and [player-frame-geometry.md](player-frame-geometry.md) |

The art sheets render to `test-reports/art/`, which is ignored by Git. They are review artefacts, and they are not part of the browser suite or its timing gate.
