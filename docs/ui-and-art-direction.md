# UI and art direction

Delivered UI and art-direction work is recorded in [archive/ui-and-art-direction-completed.md](archive/ui-and-art-direction-completed.md). This live document carries only the art and asset work that remains.

The production standard itself is [art-bible.md](art-bible.md). It is the reference, not a to-do list; what follows is what still has to be done to meet it.

The painting sequence and the exact visual package changed in each production section are tracked in [art-redesign-production-plan.md](art-redesign-production-plan.md).

## Where the art stands

Measured, not estimated. The numbers come from [asset-report.md](asset-report.md) and [player-frame-geometry.md](player-frame-geometry.md), regenerated by `npm run report:assets` and `npm run report:art`.

- 167 shipped images totalling **38.1 MB**, against a target of roughly 6 MB.
- **83** of those breach a role budget.
- **13** exact-duplicate groups and **9** orphaned assets.
- **7** backgrounds are non-uniformly stretched because their aspect is not the stage's 1.857.
- **0** of 167 shipped assets carry a recorded provenance or licence.

The player's own scale is no longer part of this list. It is calibrated per room and capped by an assertion, and is recorded in the art bible and the archive.

**Everything else's proportion still is, and is deliberately waiting on the painting.** Three human NPCs are drawn at a different scale from the player beside them — the carpenter at 2.65x, the woman who lost her mirror at 1.74x, the librarian at 0.57x — and 48 of the 64 placed sprites sit in boxes that disagree with their art's aspect, so the subject is stretched: the carpenter by -38%, the farmer by -34%, the donkey by -21%. The correction for the NPCs is computed, was verified in the running game, and is held in [bugs.md](bugs.md) against BUG-044 rather than applied, so character scale is judged once against final art instead of twice. The objects are BUG-045; NPC sprites sitting on canvases that disagree with each other are BUG-046.

## Outstanding work

### Normalise the room art

Rooms are still in several visual languages at once. The target is the painted majority; the outliers are the three hand-drawn rooms — Library Foyer, Market Street, Back Alley — and the Sewer, which is a grey photographic tunnel unlike anything else in the game. Research Room is the closest existing match to what Library Foyer has to become, so it doubles as that room's reference.

The gold standard is settled: **River Crossing** for exteriors and **Kitchen** for interiors, approved by Leigh and recorded in the art bible. The order of work is:

1. Re-compose the off-aspect backgrounds at 832 x 448 as part of each room's restyle (BUG-041). A distorted room cannot be judged for anything else, so it is done first for whichever room is being worked on.
2. Repaint the Den from the standard camera (BUG-042). It is the one room whose painted scale contradicts the rest of the game.
3. Normalise room by room against the standard, in the order the briefs give. Do not ship a scene with partially restyled elements in it.
4. Add visual baselines only once a scene is approved, so the tests protect intentional art rather than accidental inconsistency.

### Finish the player character

The Section 2 generated candidate is wired into the game first, by Leigh's direction, so it can be judged in motion before the perfection pass. It lives separately under `resources/redesign/section-02-player/`; all four directions now have nine movement poses and the original player files remain intact. It is not accepted shipping art yet: the side-frame height variance, residual keyed-green fringe, byte budgets, provenance, and licence review remain open under BUG-039/040.

The player is the most-seen asset in the game. The wired candidate now has one painted model and costume treatment; the remaining issue is production consistency between its generated poses.

Leigh has approved the **painted** finish — the one the side idles already carry — because the rooms and NPCs the game is normalising towards are painted. This reverses the earlier direction of redrawing the idles down into the flat walk finish, and moves the work from 2 frames to roughly 26.

- Paint over and matte the generated candidate against its model sheet, holding the 200x375 registration and bringing side-frame subject-height spread under 5% (BUG-039).
- Review and correct the new nine-pose front and back cycles in motion (BUG-040).
- Retain and refine the candidate's soft contact shadow so correct scaling stays grounded.

### The proportion pass, after the painting

This work is measured and ready but is held until the art it measures is final, so nothing is proportioned twice.

- Re-author the NPCs against the repainted characters, from the held values in [bugs.md](bugs.md) (BUG-044).
- Re-author every placed object's `dimensions` so its drawn box keeps its sprite's aspect, giving door and state variants their own dimensions where the two images differ in shape (BUG-045).
- Re-export each NPC's directional and state sprites onto one shared canvas per NPC, so every sprite is undistorted rather than only the active one (BUG-046).
- Converge `entityScaleAtNear` towards 1 as object dimensions are re-authored. Ten rooms still sit far from it — the Den at 3, Carpenter, Kitchen and Sewer at 2, Road Into Town at 1.8, Research Room and Dead Tree at 1.7, Library Foyer at 1.5, Cow Path at 0.8 and Market Street at 0.7 — which means an object's declared size in those rooms does not yet mean what it says.

### Bring the assets inside budget

- Re-export painted scenery as WebP. PNG is the wrong container for it and is most of the 38.1 MB.
- Resolve the 13 duplicate groups by aliasing to one semantic ID rather than shipping copies.
- Give inventory icons their own crops instead of reusing the world sprite at 660x567 for a 48 px slot.
- Decide what to do with the 9 orphans and the 31.5 MB of unshipped reference material in `resources/imageDump/`.

### Record provenance

Every shipped asset needs its `provenance` and `licence` fields filled in. The manifest leaves them `null` so an unrecorded asset stays visible; nothing ships with an unresolved licence question.

## Tools

| Command | What it produces |
| --- | --- |
| `npm run report:assets` | the manifest and [asset-report.md](asset-report.md): roles, dimensions, bytes, hashes, budget breaches, duplicates, orphans |
| `npm run check:assets` | the same, exiting non-zero on a budget breach |
| `npm run report:art` | per-room scale calibration sheets, the all-room montage, contact sheets per role, and [player-frame-geometry.md](player-frame-geometry.md) |

The art sheets render to `test-reports/art/`, which is ignored by Git. They are review artefacts, and they are not part of the browser suite or its timing gate.
